Working with your architect

You design it. We build it.

Most of the childcare centres we build are drawn by architects who have done dozens of them. Our job is to price the drawings honestly, build them exactly, and know the regulator's checklist well enough that nothing comes back after the inspection. The design stays with the architect, and so does the client's design relationship.

What we bring to your drawings

A price in days, not weeks

Send the DA set or the tender set and we price it off the drawings: a fixed lump sum with the provisional sums listed, the exclusions stated, and a program to the licensing date. Early enough that the operator's budget and your design are settled together.

Buildability comments, not redesign

Where a detail will not build the way it is drawn, or a cheaper detail does the same job, we say so as a question to you, with the dollar difference. You decide. We do not redraw your centre.

The regulator's checklist

Before pricing we read the set against the National Regulations and the Child Care Planning Guideline: unencumbered areas measured, sightlines to bathrooms and sleep rooms, nappy-change in view, shade over the outdoor area, the fence detail, the refuge if rooms are upstairs. Anything the inspection would catch, you hear about now.

The glass

Glazed walls between playrooms, into bathrooms and sleep rooms and out to the garden are our specialty: Grade A toughened and laminated glass to AS 1288, acoustic laminate where rooms meet, child-height manifestation, blinds and film where a room needs privacy, and frames detailed so a two-year-old cannot find an edge.

The wet areas

Children's bathrooms, nappy change, the art room, kitchen and laundry are where centres fail inspections and leak later. Floor wastes and falls set out before the slab, tanked walls, junior pans and basins at the heights the operator wants, and the hygiene detailing the regulator reads closely.

The outdoor area

Soft-fall to the fall heights, turf that survives, sandpits that drain, water play that does not flood the slab, shade structures engineered for wind, climb-resistant fencing and gates that self-close. Built with the landscape architect, to your plan.

How it runs

01

Concept

You send the concept and the places. We give the operator a cost range and a program so the design proceeds on a budget that holds.

02

Development application

We price the DA set and sit in on the consultant briefs where the builder's input helps: traffic for the drop-off, acoustic for the yard, the BCA report, access.

03

Construction documentation

We review the tender set for buildability and the regulator's checklist, fix the price, and agree the program with the operator's opening date.

04

Build and hand over

One site manager, weekly photo reports to you and the operator, your inspections at the stages you choose, and the file the operator needs for its service-approval inspection.

Why glass walls

The practice is named for the idea most of the best childcare designs share: a centre where nothing is hidden. Playroom to playroom, playroom to bathroom and sleep room, kitchen to dining, reception to the rooms, every room to the garden. Educators supervise without moving, parents read the whole centre from the door, and the rooms are lit from both sides.

It is the supervision rule in the National Regulations made visible, and it is the part of a childcare design that most depends on the builder: acoustic performance between rooms, safety glazing at child height, frames and sills that pass the inspection, blinds that drop to half height over the cots. We have set up to build it well.
