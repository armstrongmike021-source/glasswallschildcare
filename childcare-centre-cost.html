Childcare centre cost, Sydney 2026

What does a childcare centre cost to build?

In Sydney in 2026 a new centre costs roughly $45,000 to $60,000 for each licensed place, all in, before the land: a 90-place centre lands between $4 million and $5.5 million. Converting a building runs about $30,000 to $42,000 a place, and fitting out a shell in a new development $22,000 to $32,000. Where the money goes

The building

About 9.5 m² of building for every licensed place once the playrooms (3.25 m² a child unencumbered), cot rooms, bathrooms, kitchen, laundry, staff, office, storage and circulation are added up. A 90-place centre is roughly 850 m² of building at $4,000 to $5,800 a square metre for a new build.

The outdoor area and the site

Seven square metres a child outdoors, which for 90 places is 630 m² of soft-fall, turf, sandpit, shade and fencing at $350 to $750 a square metre, plus the car park, drop-off, driveway, landscaping and deep-soil planting, $150,000 to $250,000 on a typical site.

Design and approvals

Your architect's design at 7 to 9 per cent of the works and the development application with its planner, traffic, acoustic, access, landscape and National Quality Framework consultants at 3 to 4 per cent. Council fees and the state levy on top.

What moves the number

  • Places. Cost per place falls as the centre grows: a 120-place centre is not much dearer to run than a 90, and the kitchen, laundry, office and car park are shared across more places.
  • Age mix. Babies' rooms are the most expensive space in the building: cot rooms, nappy change, a bathroom for every eight to twelve children and the one-to-four ratio. A centre weighted to three-to-fives is cheaper to build and to staff.
  • Two storeys. Children's rooms above the ground floor need a refuge area, a second stair and a lift, and the regulator's extra assessment. Single storey is cheaper if the site allows it.
  • Basement parking. $75,000 to $110,000 a space in Sydney. A site that fits its parking at grade saves a million dollars on a 90-place centre.
  • The site. Slope, rock, trees, a flood or bushfire overlay, a main road needing acoustic treatment, or a neighbour needing a bigger setback. Every one of these is a cost before the first brick, which is why we check the site before you buy it.
  • The outdoor area on a podium. In a new development the yard is on a slab: drainage, waterproofing, fall-height fencing and the acoustic treatment for the apartments above make it the dearest outdoor area of all.

What a centre earns

Operators in Sydney charge about $150 to $200 a day for a place in 2026 and the federal Child Care Subsidy pays most of it for most families. A 90-place centre at 85 per cent occupancy turns over roughly $3 to $4 million a year. Investors pay for leased centres on yields of about 4.25 to 5.25 per cent in metropolitan Sydney, which is why a well-built centre on a 15 to 20 year lease is worth more than it cost. The federal $1 billion Building Early Education Fund, open since July 2025, and the three-day guarantee of subsidised care from January 2026 both push demand the same way: more places needed, and a shortage of sites that pass the tests.
