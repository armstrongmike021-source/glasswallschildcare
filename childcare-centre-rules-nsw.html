The rules

Everything a NSW childcare centre is designed to.

Five sets of rules shape a centre: the National Regulations that licence it, the state planning policy and guideline that approve it, the National Construction Code that builds it, and the council's controls that sit over the top. This page puts the numbers that drive the design in one place, the ones we read a set of drawings against before we price it. It is a builder's summary, not advice: the instruments themselves govern.

1. The Education and Care Services National Regulations

The rules the NSW Department of Education licenses the centre against, through the National Quality Framework. The physical ones are in Part 4.3 (regulations 103 to 117).

2. The state planning rules

Centre-based child care is governed by the State Environmental Planning Policy (Transport and Infrastructure) 2021, Chapter 3 (the old Education and Child Care SEPP) and the Child Care Planning Guideline 2021 that the consent authority must consider.

  • Every new centre is a development application. Only school-based, home-based, mobile and temporary care are exempt or complying. A conversion is a DA too. A fitout of a shell can go to a private certifier only where the consent for the building already approved the centre.
  • The indoor and outdoor space standards are non-discretionary. If the plan meets 3.25 m² and 7 m² a child, council cannot refuse on space. If it does not, the Department of Education has to concur, and usually does not.
  • The Guideline overrides the council's DCP on everything it covers except three things: building height, side and rear setbacks, and car parking rates. Those stay with the council.
  • A council cannot limit how close a new centre is to an existing one, control the age mix, or set rules beyond the National Regulations.
  • Centre-based child care is permitted with consent in the R2 low-density zone, so most of Sydney's house streets are candidates, subject to the site tests below. In industrial zones the test is the health and safety of the children.
  • The Guideline's site tests: away from petrol stations, high-voltage lines, mobile base stations, busy roads and contaminated land, or with the consultant's report that says the risk is managed. Many councils put numbers on these: a report within 500 m of a base station, within 70 m of a line over 33 kV, within 125 m of a major road.
  • Ground floor first. Children's rooms above the ground floor need the regulator's assessment and a refuge, so the Guideline and most councils push the rooms to the ground.

3. The council's controls

Each council's development control plan keeps height, setbacks and parking, and adds its own site and amenity controls. Typical Sydney figures, which vary by council:

4. The National Construction Code

A childcare centre is a Class 9b building. The Code sets the construction type, fire separation and exits, the accessible path of travel and accessible toilet, the sanitary fixtures (separate toilets for staff and children, with junior pans for the children, a kitchen, and laundry and nappy-change facilities where children under three attend), the energy-efficiency and condensation rules, and the glazing standard the glass walls are built to: Grade A safety glass to AS 1288, laminated where it has to perform acoustically.

5. The National Quality Standard

Once the centre is built the Department of Education assesses the service under the National Quality Standard. Quality Area 3, physical environment, is the one the building answers: fit for purpose, inclusive, well maintained, with indoor and outdoor spaces that let every child play, explore and be supervised. A centre designed to the table above arrives at the inspection with that area already met.
