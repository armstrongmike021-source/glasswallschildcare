Childcare centre design

Designed from the regulations outward.

A childcare centre is the most rule-bound small building in Australia. The number of children sets the floor area, the age mix sets the rooms and the bathrooms, the staff ratios set the sightlines, and the council sets the parking, the setbacks and the noise. Estier designs the centre from those numbers outward, so the plan that goes to council is the plan that gets licensed.

What the plan has to do

Space for every child

3.25 m² of unencumbered indoor space and 7 m² of unencumbered outdoor space for each child, measured without furniture, fixed equipment, passageways, bathrooms, kitchens, offices or the staff room. The licensed capacity of the centre is the smaller of what the two tests allow.

Rooms that match the ratios

In NSW one educator looks after four children under two, five children of two to three, and ten of three and over. So the rooms are sized to those multiples: a babies' room of 8 or 12, a toddlers' room of 10 or 15, preschool rooms of 20 or 30. Odd numbers waste a wage.

A bathroom for every group

Child-height toilets and basins off each playroom, visible from it, and a nappy-change bench with a sink in every room for children under three. The National Construction Code sets the count; the regulations set where they go.

Supervision by design

Regulation 115 asks for premises designed so educators can supervise children at all times. We take it literally: glass between rooms, into bathrooms and sleep rooms, low sills to the garden, no blind corners, and the verandah as the vantage point over the whole yard.

Light, air and quiet

Natural light and ventilation to every playroom, a sleep room that can be darkened, acoustic separation between rooms and from the neighbours, and the outdoor area kept under 55 dB(A) from the road where a council asks for it.

The back of house

Kitchen to food-safety standard, laundry, a cot room for the babies, staff room, office, parent reception, secure entry, storage for every room, and the bins, plant and deliveries kept away from the children's areas.

The glass-walls idea

Most centres are built as a corridor with rooms off it, each room a box with one door. The educator in the room cannot see the bathroom, the sleep room or the room next door, so every centre runs on staff walking between boxes.

Our centres are glazed. Playroom to playroom, playroom to bathroom, playroom to sleep room, kitchen to dining, reception to the rooms, every room to the garden. Full-height toughened glass, acoustic laminated where rooms meet, with blinds where a room needs to be dark or private. The result is a centre that an educator can supervise standing still, that a parent can read from the front door, and that is full of daylight from both sides.

It costs about the same as plasterboard once the acoustic glass and the blinds are counted, and it photographs far better, which matters to the operator filling the places.

Toddler room: glass to the bathroom and the nappy change, so one educator on the floor still has every child in view. Design concept.

How a centre gets designed

01

The brief

Places, age mix, hours, the operator's model (long day care, preschool, occasional care) and the budget. Everything else follows from these.

02

The site test

Zoning, area, width, slope, the road, the neighbours, power lines, flooding, bushfire and trees. Then a capacity study: how many places the site carries once parking, setbacks and the outdoor area are drawn.

03

Concept and cost

A plan and a cost on the same page. If the places and the budget do not meet, we change the plan before anything is lodged.

04

Development application

Drawings, the planner's statement, the traffic, acoustic, access and landscape reports, and the National Quality Framework checklist, lodged on the Planning Portal and followed through council or the planning panel.

Who draws it

Estier's design team draws the centre and documents it for the development application and construction. Where an operator has its own architect, we build to their drawings and bring the regulatory review, the cost control and the construction. Either way the people who price the centre are the people who build it.
