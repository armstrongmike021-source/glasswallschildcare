Childcare centre design and construction, Sydney

Childcare centres, built to your drawings.

Estier builds childcare and early-learning centres for architects, operators and developers: new centres on cleared sites, conversions of existing buildings, and fitouts of the ground floor in new developments. Your architect designs it. We price it off the drawings, build it to a fixed price, and hand it over to the operator's licensing date.

Glass walls is the idea the practice is named for. Rooms glazed to each other, to the bathrooms and sleep rooms, and to the garden: the educator never loses sight of a child, the parent at the door sees the whole centre, and the rooms fill with light. Glazed walls that have to perform acoustically and pass the regulator are a builder's problem as much as a designer's, and they are the one we have set up to solve.

Three ways to get a centre

A new centre

A cleared site of 1,500 to 4,000 m², a single-storey or two-storey building of 60 to 120 places and the outdoor area to match. Thirty-six to fifty-two weeks on site, to a fixed price off your architect's drawings.

A conversion

A house on a large block, a church hall, a shop or an office floor turned into a centre. Faster and cheaper than a new build when the building and the block pass the tests; we check them before you commit.

A fitout in a new development

The ground floor of an apartment or mixed-use building, handed over as a shell. The consent usually already names the centre, so the fitout moves fast, and the outdoor area goes on the podium.

How we work

01

Check the site

Zoning, size, slope, the road, the neighbours, power lines and parking. We tell you how many places the site can carry before you spend anything on it.

02

Check the cost

The calculator gives a Sydney range for the places you want and the way you are building, so the architect and the operator start from a budget that holds.

03

Price the drawings

Your architect designs the centre. We review the drawings for buildability and the regulator's checklist, price them, and carry the price through the development application so the approved centre is the one that was costed.

04

Build and license

One fixed price, one site manager, and the centre handed over to a program built backwards from the operator's service-approval inspection.

Why architects bring us their centres

Your design stays yours

We build what you drew. Buildability comments go back to you as questions, not as redesign, and the operator keeps dealing with you on the design. Our name goes on the hoarding as the builder, yours on the drawings.

We know the checklist

Unencumbered space, sightlines, the bathroom off every room, the nappy-change in view, the shade, the fence detail, the refuge upstairs: we read the drawings against the regulator's list before we price them, so nothing gets rebuilt after the inspection.

A price that holds

A fixed price off the drawings with the provisional sums listed, and a program built backwards from the licensing date. When the operator asks the architect what it will cost, the answer comes from the builder, in days.

Talk to us about a centre

Send us the drawings, or the address and the places if there are no drawings yet. We come back with a price, a program and a list of anything in the drawings the regulator will query, within a few days.
